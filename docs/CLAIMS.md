## Can claim (with the evidence shown)

| # | Claim | Evidence | Condition |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Atomic micro-cycles of up to 10 s save about 0.01% of the liquidity need: 0.0116% of UB, 95% CI [0.0079; 0.0152]% (10 seeds, σ = 0.4); means ≤ 0.018% across σ ∈ [0; 0.8]; it falls to ≤ 0 as volume grows to 30M payments/day; it does not depend on the maximum cycle length (2 to 10). The BCB-style potential volume measure (85% of volume) measures something else and must not be compared directly with a liquidity saving. | `robustness/seeds_summary.csv`, `robustness/scale.csv`, `robustness/cycle_len.csv` | synthetic scenario, stationary arrivals |
| 1b | No mechanism that settles each payment within d can save more than the value each participant pays in the d before its gross-settlement peak (delay bound). For d = 10 s this ceiling ranges from 0.06% to 3.3% of UB across the configurations tested (0.06–1.5% with σ calibrated to WP 619; 3.3% is the no-receiver-bias case, σ = 0), while the realised micro-cycle saving never exceeds 0.02% on average. | theorem in `METHODOLOGY.md` §9; test `engines_never_beat_the_delay_bound`; `robustness/seeds_summary.csv`, `robustness/scale.csv`, `heterogeneity/heterogeneity.csv` | general for the bound; numbers for the synthetic scenario |
| 1d | With the receiver bias calibrated to BCB WP 619 (σ = 1.0), the saving of one end-of-day netting at 30M payments/day is between 0.24% of UB (no intraday heterogeneity) and 2.8% (full class profiles); 15-minute netting saves 0.05–0.09%; second-scale netting ≈ 0. The 10 s delay bound stays ≤ 0.46% in every heterogeneity case (kill criterion K1 not fired). | `heterogeneity/heterogeneity.csv`, `bias-calibration/grid.csv` | class profile shapes are an ASSUMPTION (D18) |
| 1e | With the Conta PI's funding windows (top-ups only while the STR is open), the weekend is the binding prefunding window: about 2.7× a day's peak drawdown, and one netting at the end of the weekend would save only 0.4–1.0% of it (calibrated σ, 10M payments per Thursday); the 10 s bound inside it is ≤ 0.23% in the window form and ≤ 0.03% in the tight form (the liquidity floor inside the window; ≤ 0.10% for the Thursday night). For 15 minutes the tight bound is 1.1–1.3% of the weekend's need and for one hour 4.1–4.4%. | `funding-windows/windows.csv`; `METHODOLOGY.md` §10; test `engines_never_need_less_than_the_window_floor` | sourced hourly profile and day volumes; bilateral structure synthetic |
| 1f | The delay bound depends on how lumpy payments are relative to liquidity needs: for 10 s it is 0.76% of UB in the retail scenario but 13.7% in a stylised large-value system with 2,000 payments a day; this holds for the **window form** only. **Revised 11 Nov 2026:** the tight bound (1g) is ≤ 0.004% in the same large-value cases, so the window bound does not explain large LSM savings in large-value systems; those come from a different baseline (FIFO queue under constrained liquidity; see "cannot claim" 13). Across delays from 10 s to one day, realised netting saves 3–5% of the window bound. | `regimes/regimes.csv`, `regimes/bound_curve.csv` | large-value scenario is an ASSUMPTION |
| 1g | The liquidity floor `max_s [CO_i(s) − CI_i(s+d)]` is the least liquidity any mechanism with maximum delay d can need, per participant; `UB − floor` is never above the window bound of 1b. For 10 s, over 10 seeds, the tight bound is 0.138% of UB with σ = 0 (95% CI [0.120; 0.156]), 0.041% with σ = 0.4 ([0.025; 0.056]), 0.018% with σ = 0.8 and 0.014% with the calibrated σ = 1.0 ([0.007; 0.021]); the window form gives 0.7–3.3% for the same runs. At 30 million payments a day it is 0.024% (one seed). Netting every 10 s realises 38–46% of it and micro-cycles 13–28%; for a one-day delay it equals UB − LB. | theorem in `METHODOLOGY.md` §9b; tests `engines_never_beat_the_delay_bound`, `floor_hand_example`; `robustness/seeds_summary.csv`, `robustness/scale.csv`, `regimes/bound_curve.csv` | synthetic scenario, stationary arrivals; with class intraday profiles at 30 million payments a day the tight bound for 10 s is at most 0.042% (`heterogeneity/heterogeneity.csv`; kill criterion K1 is 2%); funding-window runs still use the window form (`specs/02`) |
| 1h | With fixed balances, designs that hold a payment for at most 10 s (queue, micro-cycles) reject the same value as gross settlement at every liquidity level tested, in retail and in a stylised large-value system: at equal service (1% of value rejected) they save at most 0.03% of liquidity in retail, with 95% CIs that include zero (large-value: 0.28%, CI [−0.30; 0.86]). Holding payments for up to one hour does save liquidity at equal service. For 1% of value rejected: 1.1–1.4% in retail with the calibrated receiver bias (σ = 1.0), 2.2–3.2% with σ = 0.4, and 3.7–5.3% in the large-value system (strict-FIFO micro-cycles: 5.3%, CI [3.5; 7.0]). For 0.1% rejected: 0.12–0.15%, 0.35–0.46% and 0.70–0.82%. The price is p95 delays of minutes to an hour when liquidity is short. | `liquidity-frontier/equivalent_liquidity.csv`, `liquidity-frontier/frontier.csv`; `METHODOLOGY.md` §11 | 10 seeds, 300,000 payments a day; balances `α × UB_i` (hindsight); rejected payments not retried; equivalent liquidity interpolated on a grid with steps of 0.01 between 0.97 and 1 (a first run with steps of 0.05 gave values up to half as large: the number is sensitive to the grid); large-value system is an ASSUMPTION |
| 1i | The per-participant liquidity floors are, in practice, the optimum of the whole system: in 120 dense small instances (20 participants, about 1,500 payments in an hour, deadlines of 2, 10 and 60 s) an offline optimiser that knows the future and may settle arbitrary batches needs exactly the sum of the floors in 116, and at most 0.3% more in the other four. The best engine realises 44–61% of that optimum. They are not equal in general (37 of 900 small random instances have a strict gap). | `optimality-gap/gap.csv`, `optimality-gap/instances.csv`; tests `sandwich_on_random_instances`, `triangle_by_hand`; `METHODOLOGY.md` §9c | small synthetic systems, not calibrated; the optimiser may split payments, so it is a lower bound on the optimum with whole payments; not checked at Pix scale (the LP does not scale) |
| 1c | Under stationary arrivals, the saving of one end-of-day netting shrinks as 1/√(volume): 9.27% of UB at 1M payments/day, 1.68% at 30M. Potential measures computed on small samples overstate it. | `robustness/scale.csv` | stationary arrivals (D8, D9) |
| 2 | Settling a lot of value inside cycles (up to 63%) does not imply saving liquidity: the liquidity peak is driven by imbalances lasting hours. | `microcycle-sweep/sweep.csv`; `robustness/seeds_summary.csv` (σ = 0.4, 10 seeds: 0.019% every 10 s, 0.50% every 15 min, 7.8% once a day) | same |
| 3 | The liquidity saving of deferred netting grows monotonically with the interval between cycles, and only one daily netting recovers the whole UB−LB gap. | `potential-vs-realizable/engines.csv` | no intraday opening balances |
| 4 | The three circularity lenses do not coincide: Hodge (diagnostic) > maximum CDFD > greedy netting, with a large gap on the net graph (0.98 / 0.17 / 0.14). | `baseline/ladder.csv`; triangle test | Hodge ≥ CDFD observed, not proven |
| 5 | The Hodge decomposition does not respect edge bounds and is not a nettable amount (triangle: 6.67 vs 5). | test `triangle_case` | general (mathematical) |
| 6 | The maximum CDFD can be computed exactly, without LP, by min-cost flow; the result equals an LP solver's on 1,000 graphs. | test `cdfd_matches_lp_oracle_on_1000_random_graphs` | general |
| 7 | Micro-cycles with a maximum delay ≥ 5 s miss the target of 50% settled within 2.8 s; with 2 s they meet it. | `microcycle-sweep/sweep.csv` | the measured delay is only the engine's |
| 8 | Under the Finnish study's logic, the instant system needs 1.1–1.5× the liquidity of one daily netting, and the gap falls to 1.01–1.03× with 48 cycles a day. | `finland-replication/ratios.csv` | depends on the bias σ |
| 9 | The simulator is bit-for-bit reproducible, on Linux x86-64, Windows x86-64 and macOS arm64 alike, with any number of worker threads, and runs on modest hardware (4th-gen i3, 4 GB). | invariant 7; CI job `golden` compares a small run of every experiment byte for byte on the three platforms and with 1 and 2 threads (`results-golden/`, `scripts/golden.py`); peak < 20 MiB per experiment | the cross-platform check covers the golden scenarios (40 participants, 30,000 payments), not the full-size runs |

## Cannot claim

| # | Forbidden claim | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | "Pix could save R$ X billion of liquidity." | Synthetic data; absolute liquidity does not scale linearly with the factor 239; LB/UB depends on an assumption (σ). |
| 2 | "X% of Pix liquidity is circular." | We only have public aggregates; the bilateral structure is an assumption. |
| 3 | "Micro-cycles do not work in real Pix." | 239× smaller scale per window; simple heuristic; not tested with real per-second volume. |
| 4 | "The BCB study overstates netting capacity." | We did not reproduce the BCB data; we reproduce the *logic* of the measure on synthetic data. |
| 5 | "We replicated Hellqvist & Korpinen." | We applied their logic to another scenario; the comparison is of orders of magnitude only. |
| 6 | "The Hodge potential × net position correlation validates the model." | On a dense graph it is almost mechanical. |
| 7 | "A large participant going offline does not affect liquidity." | Weak stress scenario (full resubmission, peak at the end of the day). |
| 8 | Any claim about the proportional CDFD variant or about on-chain data. | Not implemented / not run. |
| 10 | "Potential liquidity saving is 9.27%" or "realizable is 630× smaller than potential". | The potential depends on scale (9.27% at 1M payments/day, 1.68% at 30M) and on the seed (σ = 0.4: CI [4.5; 11.1]%); the ratio is not a stable number (`robustness/`). |
| 11 | Any single-seed number as a result. | Use the 10-seed means and CIs of `robustness/seeds_summary.csv`. |
| 12 | "The Hodge curl share shows that X% of the flow is circular." | On the baseline net graph the random-flow null value is (m − n + 1)/m = 0.970 against 0.977 observed (`METHODOLOGY.md` §9). |
| 13 | "LoopSettle reproduces / refutes McMahon et al. (2024)." | Different systems (LVTS and T2, large-value) and, for T2, a different baseline (FIFO with queues under constrained liquidity). The LVTS paper's measure (aggregate mNDP) equals our UB, but on real large-value data we do not have; *Updated 11 Nov 2026:* `liquidity-frontier` now simulates a queue under constrained liquidity and finds the same direction (long holds save more in the lumpy large-value system than in retail: 3.7–5.3% against 1.1–1.4%), but on a stylised system, with a one-hour deadline instead of their batching, and with magnitudes far from their 14–30%; it is not a reproduction. |
| 9 | "FedNow / TIPS / UPI would save X% of liquidity." | The scenario is calibrated to Pix only; other systems need their own public aggregates. The engines and the qualitative mechanism apply ([`INSTANT_PAYMENTS.md`](INSTANT_PAYMENTS.md)); the numbers do not carry over. |
